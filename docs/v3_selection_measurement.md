**The v2 substrate is the best of the five on the primary score.** The two feedback

## 2. The eigenmode diagnostic explains the failure

S2's participation ratio is **37.96–41.67 across all five candidates** — the input reaches
~40 of A's modes in every candidate. So the failure is *not* "the input misses the modes":
`B` couples to many modes, and the Krylov effective rank still saturates at ≈7 of a possible
85 (capacity used 0.055–0.084). The modes `B` reaches are largely **not independent under
propagation** — which is the eigenmode-accessibility statement from M5, now reproduced across
five biologically distinct substrates.

## 3. A measurement caveat, recorded rather than hidden

The unit tests caught that the **pre-registered S1 is not monotone in `K`**: the singular
values of the concatenated block span ≈ρ^K, so at K=16 the participation ratio is dominated
by whichever power has the largest norm. The scorer therefore also reports
`numerical_rank_relative` (tolerance relative to σ_max), the per-power norms `‖A^k B‖_F`, and
the capacity diagnostics — so the selection's sensitivity to the definition is visible. **The
pre-registered S1 was left exactly as signed**; the extras are diagnostics, not a
redefinition. This does not change the verdict: the failure is by a factor of 1.4 against the
gate, and S0's S1 was already known to be ≈5–7 from M5/M5b's independent measurements.

## 4. Three generator bugs found by running it, all reported

The selection ran three times before producing the numbers above; each earlier run was
recorded rather than silently re-run:

1. **Input support was zero for every candidate** — `CandidateSubstrate` carried row indices
   while the annotation lookup is keyed by root id, so every node resolved to `<absent>` and
   `W_in` came back empty (S1 = 0.0). Fixed by carrying `root_ids`.
2. **The feedback closure was inverted**, so S1/S3/S4 raised *"the declared feedback closure
   admitted no node"*: the condition required the closing edge's source to be of a target
   class. Fixed to admit a source-class node whose real edge lands into the base set.
3. **The cap let S0 crowd the feedback out entirely** — S0 already fills `target_n=1000`, so
   keeping S0's nodes first left S1/S3/S4 bit-identical to S0 (M=80,443, S1=7.129 for all
   three). Fixed by ranking the union on the declared frontier score, so feedback neurons
   compete with S0's weakest frontier nodes. The provenance records the effect:
   **95 / 237** (S1), **192 / 426** (S3), **212 / 566** (S4) S0-nodes displaced / feedback
   nodes admitted.

Bug 3 matters for the verdict: **a stop-loss reached while three of five candidates were
degenerate copies of the first would not have been a test of the candidate set.** The
stop-loss above is on five genuinely distinct substrates.

## 5. What the stop-loss means, and what it does not

**It means** (per pre-registration §6): the connectome-reservoir main line stops; no v4 is
opened; the paper question changes instead. The accumulated record — v2's construct failure
(C3), M5 (the substrate *has* the cycles), M5b (input support excluded), v3 (five
biologically-distinct substrates, none reaching 2·Din) — is the answer to *"can the
Drosophila olfactory connectome serve as a recurrent reservoir for e-nose sensing?"* on this
construction family.

**It does not mean** that a better substrate cannot exist in principle, nor that the
connectome is computationally uninteresting. What is established is narrower and stronger:
**across the declared biologically-defined candidate family, the input's Krylov reach
saturates at ≈7 of 85 available dimensions, and the variants that add feedback or edges do
not improve it.** No claim of biological superiority attaches to any candidate — S0 simply
scored highest, which says nothing about biological fidelity.

**Diagnostic-only status of S3.** As pre-registered, the selection engine never read an S3
field, and a test asserts that both behaviourally and by checking the engine's source never
names one. S3's values (ρ=0.851, Henrici non-normality 0.130–0.153) are reported for
mechanism, not for ranking.

## 6. Where DrosoSense-RC goes from here

Per the owner's locked decision tree, the honest next step is a **change of paper question**,
not another substrate redesign. The exit route (a "fixed connectome-constrained sensory
kernel" rather than a reservoir) remains available but is **not** a main line: the project
already has `H < X` (audit A2r/A4r) and weak state expansion, and renaming does not improve
either.

What the project now holds, and can be written up honestly:

* a **rigorous construct-validation methodology** for connectome-computing claims (C1–C4,
  evidence identity, the cluster-unit amendment), demonstrated on a real system;
* a **measured, mechanism-level negative** for the reservoir framing: the olfactory
  connectome's recurrence does not produce a controllable, memory-bearing state under a
  faithful input geometry, across five biologically distinct substrates;
* the **structural diagnostics** that make that negative checkable (SCC/cycle analysis,
  Krylov controllability, eigenmode accessibility) — reusable for any future
  connectome-substrate proposal.
